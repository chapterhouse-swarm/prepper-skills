# Emergency procedures

Checked 2026-11-18. Operating practice based on 47 CFR 97.119 (identification), 97.401-97.407 (emergency communications) and common net practice. Formal message forms (ICS-213, ARRL Radiogram) are described here in summary only; get the official forms from FEMA (ICS-213) or your local ARES group.

## Distress call (life-threatening)

Speak slowly. Repeat the key facts.

> "**Mayday, Mayday, Mayday.** This is [call sign, or your name]. I am at [location: address, crossroads, GPS coordinates or grid]. [What happened]. [Number of people, injuries]. I need [help needed]. Over."

Then listen. If nobody answers, repeat every few minutes on the same frequency, then try others in `common-frequencies.md`. Use "Mayday" only for immediate danger to life.

For urgent but not life-threatening needs, say "**Emergency traffic**" or "**Priority traffic**" at the start instead.

## Joining a net

1. Listen until the net control station (NCS) asks for check-ins.
2. Give your call sign, phonetically: "Kilo Four Alpha Bravo Charlie".
3. Say "with traffic" only if you have a message to pass.
4. Wait to be recognized. Pass messages only when NCS directs you.
5. Do not chat. Emergency nets are for traffic.

## Identification (amateur)

Give your call sign at the end of each communication and at least every 10 minutes during a contact. In English, voice or CW.

## Welfare message (what to write down)

Keep it short and complete. Fields that every format shares:
- **To:** name, phone or address
- **From:** name, call sign, location
- **Message:** 25 words or fewer when possible ("All safe at home. Power out. Need nothing. Will call when phones return.")
- **Time and date** sent, and the frequency/station that took it.

Read the message back word for word with the receiving station before you say it's sent.

## Logging

Write down every contact or message: time (local and UTC if you can), frequency, station, summary. Logs help served agencies and protect you.

## Power and equipment

- Keep radios charged; carry spare batteries or a charging cable for a vehicle (vehicle outdoors only).
- Use low power first; it saves battery and is required (minimum power necessary).
- A better antenna helps more than more power: elevate it, or use a roll-up or mag-mount antenna.
