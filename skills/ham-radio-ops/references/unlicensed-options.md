# Options without an amateur license

Checked 2026-11-18. Sources: FCC FRS, MURS, CB and GMRS pages; 47 CFR Parts 95 and 97; NWS (NOAA Weather Radio). See `sources.md`.

## Always legal: listen

- **NOAA Weather Radio** on 162.400-162.550 MHz (7 channels). Official warnings.
- **Scanners and shortwave receivers:** listening to amateur, public broadcast, and most public-safety voice is legal in most places. Some states restrict scanners in vehicles; check local law.

## Talk without a license

| Service | License | Power | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FRS** (Family Radio Service) | None (licensed by rule) | 2 W on ch 1-7 and 15-22; 0.5 W on ch 8-14 | 22 channels shared with GMRS. Fixed antenna, no repeaters. Typical range: neighborhood. |
| **MURS** | None (licensed by rule) | 2 W | 151.820, 151.880, 151.940, 154.570, 154.600 MHz. External antennas allowed, so range can beat FRS. |
| **CB** | None | 4 W AM; 12 W PEP SSB | 40 channels at 27 MHz. **Channel 9 is for emergencies and traveler assistance.** Channel 19 is used by highway traffic. |

## Easy license, no exam: GMRS

- $35 for 10 years, no test, apply through the FCC's licensing system. Covers the licensee's immediate family.
- Up to 50 W on the main channels, and repeaters are allowed, so range is far better than FRS.
- Many GMRS users monitor channel 20 (462.675 MHz) for emergencies by informal convention; it is not an official emergency channel.

## The next step: Technician license

An exam of 35 questions, given by volunteer examiners, many online. Unlocks local repeaters, 2 m/70 cm simplex, and the emergency nets.

## Genuine life-threatening emergency

Part 97's emergency rules (97.403 and 97.405) say that nothing in the rules prevents an **amateur station** from using any means available when there is an immediate threat to life or property and normal communications are not available. They are written for amateur stations. They are often read as also covering an unlicensed person calling for help in a true emergency, but the rules don't say that directly, and this is not legal advice.

Practical guidance, in order:
1. Try 911 by phone and text, then FRS/GMRS/MURS/CB channel 9.
2. Only if a life is at immediate risk and nothing else works: a brief call for help on any radio at hand is the kind of use these provisions exist to protect. Keep it to the emergency (see the distress script in `emergency-procedures.md`), and stop transmitting once help is reached.
3. Never use this as a reason to transmit for convenience, news, or checking in.
