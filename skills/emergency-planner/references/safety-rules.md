# Hard safety rules

Always include the ones that apply to the plan. Checked 2026-11-18. Sources: CDC and CPSC (carbon monoxide), ESFI (downed lines), Ready.gov, NWS. See `sources.md`.

## Carbon monoxide (outages, winter storms)

- **Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or charcoal inside** a home, garage, basement, crawl space or tent, even with doors or windows open.
- Run generators outdoors, **at least 20 feet** from doors, windows and vents.
- Never heat the home with a gas oven or stovetop.
- Have a working battery CO alarm. Headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion in several people at once: **get everyone into fresh air and call 911.**

## Fire

- Candles: prefer flashlights. Never leave a flame unattended.
- Space heaters: keep anything that can burn well away from them, plug directly into a wall outlet, turn off when sleeping or leaving.
- Store fuel outdoors in approved containers, away from the house.

## Electrical

- Do not plug a generator into a wall outlet ("backfeeding"); it can kill utility workers. Use a transfer switch installed by an electrician, or run cords to appliances.
- Stay at least 35 feet away from downed power lines and anything touching them; report them to the utility or 911.

## Water and flooding

- **Turn Around, Don't Drown.** Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater.
- Floodwater can carry sewage, chemicals and live electricity.

## Medical

- People who rely on powered medical devices or refrigerated medication need a plan before the power goes out: backup power, or a place to go. Contact the utility and the provider in advance.
- This skill does not diagnose or dose. For injuries: call 911 if reachable; otherwise follow a printed first-aid guide from an official source.
