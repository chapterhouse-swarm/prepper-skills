# Water

Checked 2026-11-18. Sources: Ready.gov (Water), CDC "Making Water Safe in an Emergency", EPA "Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water". See `sources.md`.

## How much

- Plan **1 gallon per person per day** for drinking and basic hygiene.
- Children, nursing mothers, and people who are sick need more; hot weather can double the need.
- Ready.gov suggests at least several days' supply; plan for longer at home if you can (two weeks is a common home target).
- Pets need water too; plan their share separately.

## Making water safe (in this order of preference)

1. **Use stored or bottled water** first.
2. **Boil:** bring clear water to a rolling boil for **1 minute** (**3 minutes above 6,500 ft**). Let it cool. If cloudy, let it settle and filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter first.
3. **Unscented household bleach** (only if the label says 5-6% or 8.25% sodium hypochlorite; no scented, color-safe or "splash-less" bleach):

| Water | 5-6% bleach | 8.25% bleach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 quart / 1 liter | 2 drops | 2 drops |
| 1 gallon | 8 drops | 6 drops |
| 2 gallons | 16 drops (1/4 tsp) | 12 drops (1/8 tsp) |
| 4 gallons | 1/3 tsp | 1/4 tsp |
| 8 gallons | 2/3 tsp | 1/2 tsp |

- **Cloudy, colored or very cold water: double the amount** (after filtering).
- Stir and wait **at least 30 minutes**. It should have a slight chlorine smell. If not, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes.
- Bleach loses strength over time; an old bottle may not work. If the concentration is unknown or not in the table, **boil instead**.

## Never

- Never use water you believe is contaminated with fuel or toxic chemicals; boiling and bleach do not fix that. Get water elsewhere.
- Never drink from water beds, radiators, or pool/spa water (cleaning chemicals).
