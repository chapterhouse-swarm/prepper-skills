# Food safety in an outage

Checked 2026-11-18. Source: USDA FSIS "Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies". See `sources.md`.

## Cold storage without power (doors kept closed)

| Appliance | Keeps food safe for about |
|---|---|
| Refrigerator | **4 hours** |
| Full freezer | **48 hours** |
| Half-full freezer | **24 hours** |

- Keep doors closed. Group frozen packages together so they stay cold.
- If the outage will run past 4 hours, move perishables to a cooler with ice or frozen gel packs, at 40°F or below.
- An appliance thermometer settles the question: refrigerator 40°F or below, freezer 0°F or below.

## After the outage

- **Discard** perishable refrigerated food (meat, poultry, fish, eggs, leftovers, deli items, soft cheese, milk) that went more than 4 hours without power.
- Frozen food that still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below can be refrozen or cooked.
- **Never taste food to decide if it is safe. When in doubt, throw it out.**
- Discard food that touched floodwater unless it is in a sealed, undamaged commercial can (clean and sanitize the can first).

## Planning calories

The 2,000 kcal per person per day figure in `scripts/supply_calc.py` is a planning assumption, not an official requirement. Adjust it for the household (children, physical work, cold weather).
